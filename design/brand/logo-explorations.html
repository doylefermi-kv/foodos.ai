FoodOS.ai — logo explorations

Three routes to a mark that has to work as a 16px favicon, a 76px app icon, a shop-board sign and a 1200×630 link preview. All three use the same hand-drawn FoodOS.ai wordmark, so the only variable on this page is the symbol.

Product Designer · 13 Oct 2026 · all artwork hand-authored SVG, no fonts, no CDNs

Decided: Concept 01, “Chit” — CTO, 13 Oct 2026. The page below is the exploration as it was argued, including my recommendation of Concept 02. Jump to the decision note for what changed and what I got wrong.

The wordmark (fixed across all three)

Drawn on a 100-unit cap height with a 24-unit stem — a black-weight grotesque with flat-sided round letters, which is how hand-painted Indian shop boards resolve a curve. The .ai keeps the marigold sticker treatment from the approved site header: it is the one piece of the identity that says what the product is, so it gets a box around it.

Decided — Concept 01, “Chit”  ·  CTO, 13 Oct 2026

The CTO picked Concept 01, over my recommendation of 02. The final assets in this folder are all Chit. Everything below is left as written — it is the record of what I argued and why, not a description of what shipped.

Two things I got wrong in that argument, found while drawing the final set:

  • “Partly — bill lines merge” was a property of my drawing, not of the concept. Widening the body from 56 to 64 units and taking the bars from 11 to 13 holds both bars open at 16px with the torn edge still legible. See icons-check.html — that is a real 16px render, not a downscale.
  • It needs no second cut. The question I raised about two source files was asked of Thali, whose spoon bowl genuinely fails small. Chit does not have that problem, so there is one drawing at every size — and export.mjs now fails the export if the three files carrying it ever drift apart.
As explored
As shipped

Same mark, bigger inside the tile and heavier in the bars. The browser is drawing these live from the same path as logo-mark.svg; for the true rasterised 16px, see icons-check.html.

The “receipts are the default POS mark” objection stands and is not solved by drawing it better. What offsets it: the teeth are coarse and asymmetric where the category draws fine perforations, and the mark is a filled ink tile rather than a floating outline, which is where most of the 16px distinctiveness comes from.

Why Thali. It is the only one of the three that survives the 16px test without losing its idea, and the only one that earns the OS in the name rather than decorating around it. The Indian-ness is in the object itself — a steel plate with a rim — not in an applied pattern, which is the brief for Direction C. It also gives us somewhere to go: the ring is a frame, so each product module can take a different centre (a chit for KOT, a crate for inventory, a bar for reports) without a new logo.

What I'd carry over from the others. Concept 01's torn chit is too good to throw away — it should stay as an illustration motif on the site (section dividers, the pricing card, the KOT screenshot frame), just not as the logo.

Open question for the CTO. The simplified ≤24px cut means the favicon is a slightly different drawing from the large mark. That's normal practice, but it does mean two source files to keep in sync. Shout if you'd rather we use one drawing everywhere and accept a softer 16px.

Answered: Chit, and one drawing everywhere. Both are reflected in the shipped files — and with Chit the trade-off in that question disappears, because there is no softer 16px to accept.
